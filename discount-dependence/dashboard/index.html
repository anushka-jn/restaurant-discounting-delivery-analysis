Discount Dependence Analysis

Which restaurants convert discount into real traction, and which are just leaking margin. Built on 139,319 real Swiggy restaurant listings across 581 Indian cities and 117 cuisines — cleaned, feature-engineered, and analysed entirely in SQL (DuckDB).

Limitations

  • Reverse causality is plausible. Struggling restaurants may discount more because they're struggling, not the other way round. This analysis locates the cells worth testing — it does not prove discounting causes low traction. Fix: randomise offer caps across matched city×cuisine cells and measure order volume + partner churn over 8 weeks.
  • Rating count is a demand proxy, not order volume — and it's scraped as a coarse floor (10+, 20+, 50+, 100+, 500+, 1K+, 5K+, 10K+; only 15 distinct levels across 139K restaurants). The median collapses onto one bucket for most groups, so every chart here uses the mean, which still reflects how the underlying mix shifts.
  • Discount depth for flat-₹ offers is implied, not measured. Percent-off offers give a real percentage; flat-₹ offers (e.g. "FLAT ₹120 OFF") don't carry one, so depth is estimated as flat_amount ÷ cost_for_two. This is a stated modelling assumption.
  • 42% of restaurants (59,099) sit in peer groups smaller than 15 and are excluded from every peer-relative comparison (efficiency index, quadrant tiering, leakage sizing, recommendation table) — thin cells were dropped rather than trusted.
  • This dataset carries no free-delivery offers — only percent-off, flat-₹, and a small number of BOGO deals (<0.1% of restaurants) — so the "free delivery vs % off" comparison from the original brief doesn't apply here; BOGO's rarity is reported instead.
Pipeline: DuckDB SQL (staging → cleaning → offer-text parsing → feature engineering → analysis), orchestrated with a Python/duckdb runner. Raw offer text (e.g. "20% OFF UPTO ₹100\nUSE CODE...ABOVE ₹149") parsed with regex into offer type, discount %, cap, and minimum order.
